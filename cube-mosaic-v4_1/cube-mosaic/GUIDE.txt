CUBE MOSAIC GENERATOR - GUIDE
=============================

What the program does
---------------------
It turns a photo into a wall of Rubik's cubes. Every cube shows one face, and every sticker on that face is one
"pixel" of the picture. Only the six cube colors are used, and every sticker position always gets one of them.

Typical workflow
----------------
1. Photo: choose the picture.
2. Cube and size: pick the cube type and the maximum space (meters).
3. Press Generate. A pop-up shows the result and asks "Is this good?".
4. In the pop-up: look at the views, fix details with flips, then Accept (saves the files), Adjust (back to the
   settings; your flips are kept) or Cancel.

THE SETTINGS WINDOW
-------------------

Photo
  Any png, jpg, jpeg and similar files. Phone photos are rotated the right way up automatically. If the picture does
  not have exactly the shape of the cube grid, a thin strip is cropped (about 7% at most when the program can help it);
  the summary prints how much.

Cube and size
  Cube type      2x2 to 7x7. Choosing one loads its usual size. More stickers per cube = finer detail per cube.
  Cube size      Edge length in mm. Brands differ by a millimeter or two; set yours.
  Sticker size   The colored square. Making stickers smaller shows more black frame, so every color looks darker.
  Padding        The frame gap = pitch minus sticker. Sticker and padding change each other; edit either.
  Pitch          Cube size divided by the cube type: the distance from one sticker center to the next.
  Maximum width/height   The space you have, in meters. The program picks the biggest grid of whole cubes that fits.
                         A larger space means more cubes and more detail.

Sticker colors (hex)
  The six colors as your cubes really are (standard Rubik's colors by default). Click the swatch for a color picker.
  A red border around a swatch means the text is not a valid hex code.
  The program matches the photo against how the colors LOOK from a distance, which is a blend of sticker and frame,
  not against the raw hex codes. So matching your real colors matters.

Frame
  Opaque                  Solid plastic (usually black). Pick its color.
  Translucent             Opacity and "color seen through it". A translucent white frame over a black core looks gray.
  Frameless / stickerless The colored plastic is the pixel. Only a thin seam shows: set its width and color.
  Light frames make colors look lighter and less contrasty; black frames make them darker and richer.

Viewing distance
  Tick "Use the recommended distance", or untick it and type where people will stand (meters).
  The program works out a comfortable range from the mosaic size (the whole picture should fill roughly 20 to 60
  degrees of view, best about 40). If your distance falls outside it, the pop-up shows an orange note with the
  recommended distance. The note also says whether neighbouring stickers will visually merge from your distance.

Color matching and dithering
  Lightness weight   1 = plain. Lower (0.4 to 0.7) makes the program care less about light/dark and more about
                     which color family the photo has.
  Hue guard          0 = off. At 70, a colorful part of the photo may only get a sticker within 70 degrees of its hue.
                     This is the main cure for blue things turning green. Light blues become white instead.
  Guard ignores grays below chroma   Near-grays have no real hue; below this value the guard stays out of the way.
  Per-color nudge    One box per color, in the order W Y R O B G. A positive number makes that color used LESS,
                     a negative one makes it used MORE. 0.002 is gentle, 0.01 strong.
                     "All targets" applies everywhere; "Near-gray targets only" only to grays.
                     Grays have no matching sticker, and mid-grays tend to turn green. Penalising green for grays
                     sends them to orange or red instead, so it is a taste choice.
                     A very large nudge only means "never use this color"; the replacement is whatever is next best,
                     not necessarily the color you wanted. For an exact swap use the flip tools below.
  Dithering          Mixes neighbouring colors so smooth gradients look smooth from far away. Automatic follows the
                     viewing distance: none when stickers are clearly visible (close up it would just look grainy),
                     full when they blend together (far away). You can force Off, Full, or set the strength yourself.

Output folder
  Where Accept writes the files (see the end of this guide).

THE "IS THIS GOOD?" POP-UP
--------------------------
Note at the top      Green = your viewing distance is fine, orange = it clashes (with the recommended distance),
                     slate = no distance given.
Views                Build view: real stickers on the frame, as it will be built.
                     From far: each sticker blended with its frame, roughly the look from a distance.
                     Original photo: the cropped photo, for comparing.
                     Original | From far: both side by side.
                     Flip changes: only the stickers changed by your flip rules are bright.
Zoom and move        Mouse wheel zooms around the pointer. Hold the right (or middle) button and drag to move the
                     picture. The Zoom +, Zoom - and Fit buttons do the same without a wheel.
                     When zoomed in, thin lines show stickers and thick lines show cubes.
Reading the picture  Move the pointer over the mosaic: the line under it names the sticker, its current color and
                     the cube column and row.
Selecting            Drag with the left button to select a rectangle, or click to select one sticker. The selection
                     always snaps to whole stickers, so what is highlighted is exactly what a flip will change.
                     Rectangles can be drawn in every view except the side-by-side one.

Exact color flip / paint (right side of the pop-up)
  Pick a source color, a target color, optionally select an area, and press Add flip.
  X becomes Y   Every sticker of color X in the area becomes Y. Nothing else moves.
  Any color     Choose "Any color (paint)" as the source to paint the whole selection with the target color,
                whatever it was. Select a single sticker to repaint just that one.
  No area       Without a selection the rule covers the whole mosaic.
  Swapping      B becomes G together with G becomes B swaps the two colors. Rules never read each other's results,
                so B becomes G followed by G becomes R does NOT turn blue into red.
  Overlaps      If two rules claim the same sticker, the later one in the list wins.
  Switching off Untick a rule to ignore it, or remove it. Sticker counts and the saved files always use the result
                AFTER the flips.
  Keep in mind  Flip rectangles are in sticker coordinates. If you change the cube type or the size limits, the grid
                changes and old rectangles no longer match the picture.

SAVED FILES (Accept)
--------------------
preview.png             Mosaic with the viewing-distance note on top.
preview_clean.png       The mosaic alone.
perceived.png           The blended "from far" look.
indices.npy             The answer: one number per sticker (0 W, 1 Y, 2 R, 3 O, 4 B, 5 G), flips included.
indices_before_flips.npy, flips.txt   Only when flips exist: the result before flips, and the rules in command-line form.

COMMAND LINE (same engine as the GUI)
-------------------------------------
python -m cubemosaic.cli photo.jpg --n 3 --max-w-m 2 --max-h-m 1.5 --hue-guard-deg 70 --flip B=G --compare
python -m cubemosaic.diagnose --hue-guard-deg 70      (shows which sticker each hue and lightness will get)
--flip X=Y   --flip G=B@x0,y0,x1,y1   --flip "*=R@10,5,11,6" (paints one sticker; quote the *)

GOOD STARTING POINTS
--------------------
* Blue things turn green: hue guard 70.
* Grays turn green: nudge near-gray G to about 0.02 and see which cast you prefer.
* Result looks noisy up close: set dithering to Off, or use the automatic setting with the real viewing distance.
* Result looks flat and banded far away: dithering Full.
* One small area is wrong: zoom in, select it, flip it or paint it.
